# Write — LinkedIn

Turn the **locked master content** for **$ARGUMENTS** into a LinkedIn carousel and caption. LinkedIn is the *visual* channel — the carousel is the content, posted as a **PDF document**, and a design tool polishes it afterward, so deliver clean slide-by-slide text plus a short design note.

## Preconditions & change protocol (read first)

- **Content first.** Only render once the master content is locked and the person has approved it (Phase 1 / **explore**). If it isn't, stop and finalize the content first.
- **One spine, faithful rendering.** This is the same thesis as the Substack essay and Medium article, rendered for LinkedIn — it reinforces, never duplicates. Never reuse the Substack prose as slides.
- **Same running example** as every other asset.
- **Change protocol.** If the person asks to change the substance after this exists, don't patch it here — update the master content (**explore**), re-approve, and regenerate all platform assets (**refine** runs the re-sync).
- **Match the person's voice** — preserve their phrasing and any strong line they already wrote.

Save the output as a Markdown file so it can go straight to a design tool. The slides themselves get exported as a **PDF** for the LinkedIn document post — the LinkedIn portion of the **design-brief** artifact specifies that PDF slide-by-slide.

## The carousel (≈8–11 slides)

Uploaded as a PDF "document" post.

1. **Hook** — one sharp claim that opens a question, plus "→" to pull the swipe. Tease the payoff; don't spoil it. Avoid any framing that contradicts the thesis.
2. **Build slides** — one idea each, walking the argument; give each a bold lead-in label so the deck scans at a glance.
3. **Insight slide** — name the non-obvious through-line ("the aha").
4. **Turn / payoff** — the boldest or most surprising step, framed as the natural culmination; visually distinct from the build slides.
5. **Mental-model slide** — a single quotable line (the slide people screenshot).
6. **Where it goes** — the forward-looking implication.
7. **CTA** — a short prompt plus an open question ("What rung are you building on?").

Keep slides to a few short lines; they must be readable on a phone.

**Design note to include for the design tool:** slide count; one accent color reserved for the hero element of the story; consistent neutrals elsewhere; build slides share a motif; the turn slide breaks that rhythm. If the framing is positive/evolutionary, explicitly say "no warning aesthetics (red, cracks, alert icons)." For the full slide-by-slide PDF spec, use the **design-brief** skill.

## The caption

The text above the carousel:

- **Hook line** consistent with slide 1.
- **2–4 short prose paragraphs** expanding the arc (single-sentence paragraphs and line breaks read well in-feed).
- **"Swipe through 👇"** to point at the deck.
- **CTA to the long-form** ("Full essay on Substack — link in the comments") and an **open question** to invite replies.
- **4–5 specific hashtags**, no stuffing.

## Caption hygiene (this is where reach is won or lost)

- **External links suppress reach in the post body.** Put the link in the *first comment* and say "link in the comments." Post the carousel, then immediately add the comment.
- **A framed first comment beats a bare URL** — one line that sells the click ("Wrote the long version here, where I get into [the spicy implication] 👇").
- **Strip tracking params** (`?utm_...`, `?r=...`, share flags) from any shared link.

For *when* to post and the cross-posting funnel, hand off to the distribute skill.
