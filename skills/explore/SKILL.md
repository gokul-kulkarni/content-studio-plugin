# Explore, Frame & Lock the Content

Sharpen the raw idea in **$ARGUMENTS** into something worth publishing, *before* a single platform post is written. The job here is not to format what was handed over — it's to make the idea sharper, decide the strongest way to tell it, and produce a **locked master content** that every platform will later render.

This skill owns **Phase 1** of the pipeline and the **approval gate**: no LinkedIn/Substack/Medium asset is generated until the person confirms the content is good.

Work the passes in order.

## Pass 0 — Assumptions & clarification (do this first, every time)

Before sharpening anything, surface what you're assuming and what you're unsure about. Readers and authors lose time when an unspoken assumption turns out wrong.

- **State assumptions explicitly.** List the things you're taking as given — audience, platform(s), level (beginner vs expert), tone (optimistic vs critical), length, whether it's part of a series, and any technical premise you're trusting. Flag each as an assumption, not a fact.
- **Ask only the questions that change the output.** If something genuinely forks the work (e.g. "is this for a general audience or engineers?"), ask it now rather than guessing. Keep it to the few that matter; don't interrogate.
- **Offer sensible defaults.** For anything non-blocking, pick the obvious default, say what you picked, and move on — let the person correct it.

Present the assumptions and questions, and let the person respond before going deep.

## Pass 1 — Technical accuracy

Read the claims as a knowledgeable peer would. Gently correct anything imprecise, and *strengthen* the argument rather than merely fact-checking it. Frame it collaboratively — "you're right, with N precision tweaks" — never as a takedown. A wrong technical detail in a published post is a real credibility cost, so this pass matters. If the person invited correction, lean in confidently.

## Pass 2 — Framing

Find the single strongest angle and surface it explicitly. The raw framing is often weaker than what the material can support. Watch for three traps:

- **Self-undercutting framing** — a hook that contradicts the thesis (e.g. calling something "the same story repeated" when the whole point is that the last step breaks the pattern).
- **Loss-framed when it could be forward-looking** — a negative/ominous angle where an evolutionary, momentum-forward one is truer to intent. Default to optimistic unless the person wants critique or warning — but confirm, don't impose.
- **Pedantry-bait** — hard dates, exact figures ("70 years"), contestable specifics that invite "well, actually" replies. Strip them unless load-bearing; timeless framing ("always," "every leap") is stronger and unarguable.

## Output: the spine + the locked content

End with two things:

1. **The spine** — one or two sentences capturing the thesis arc, the through-line every later asset will share. A useful shape: *"Every step in X traded A to gain B; the newest step is the boldest version of that move; and we're already maturing it."*
2. **The master content** — the finalized, platform-neutral substance: the key beats in order, the one **running example** to use throughout, any code/figures, and (if part of a series) the call-back to the previous piece and the tease for the next. This is the single source of truth all platforms render from.

Pick **one concrete running example** and note it explicitly — every platform and every code snippet must reuse the *same* example so the package feels coordinated.

## The approval gate (do not skip)

State clearly that this is the content-finalization phase, then **stop and get explicit approval** before any platform post is generated. Only once the person says the content is good do the **write-linkedin**, **write-substack**, and **write-medium** skills render it.

If the person later asks to change the content (even after posts exist), come back here: update the master content, re-confirm, then have every platform asset regenerated so they stay in sync. Never let a content change live in only one platform.
